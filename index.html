// what it is

A real energy optimiser that lives in your house.

forty-two watts (42W) is an open-source, local-first home energy management system. One small Go binary runs on a Raspberry Pi and turns your solar inverter, home battery, grid meter and EV charger into one coordinated system — deciding every few seconds when to charge, discharge, hold, import or export, so your house quietly runs on its own power and the cheapest electrons on the spot market.

The usual home setup

  • Every vendor has its own app and its own cloud
  • Battery, solar and EV charger don't talk to each other
  • Control round-trips through a data centre — 2–5 s of latency
  • "Optimisation" is a timer with a schedule
  • It stops working the day the cloud does

With 42W

  • One binary coordinates every device on your site
  • PV, load, battery and the car are planned together
  • A real control loop on the edge — decisions in milliseconds
  • A genuine 48-hour model-predictive optimiser, not a schedule
  • Runs fully offline; the cloud is opt-in, never required

We haven't found another open project that does all of this at once — a real model-predictive optimiser, a millisecond edge control loop, V2X-aware dispatch and a hot-reloadable driver model — running locally on a $50 computer under an MIT licence. If 42W isn't the state of the art in open home energy, point us at what is.

// what it does

Three loops. One binary.

42W is three control layers compiled into one static executable. Each has its own job and its own clock. Together they keep the lights on even when the forecasts lie.

Control loop 5 s

PI controller with anti-windup (3 kW integral limit), Kalman-smoothed meter signal, seven independent safety clamps — fuse guard, slew rate, SoC floor/ceiling, saturation envelope, dispatch holdoff. The part that keeps the lights on.

MPC planner 15 min · 48 h

Dynamic programming over 51 SoC levels × 21 actions × 193 slots. Solves in ~0.6 ms. Three strategies: self-consumption, passive arbitrage, full arbitrage. Reactive replan on forecast divergence > 500 Wh.

Self-learning forecasts PV · load · price

Built-in PV and load forecasting that teaches itself — no setup, no training phase, no external service. A PV twin learns your roof, a load twin learns your household (down to how it heats), and a price twin fills the gap past the day-ahead. They get better every day they run.

Negative prices, handled

When the spot price drops below zero, exporting costs you money. 42W knows — it stops dumping to the grid and soaks the cheap (or paid-for) energy into the battery or the car instead. A penalty becomes a free charge.

Lua drivers vast majority

Covers the vast majority of inverters, batteries and chargers people actually own — Sungrow, Ferroamp, Huawei, Deye, Solis, SMA, Fronius, Kostal, GoodWe, Growatt, Sofar, SolarEdge, Victron, Pixii, sonnen, Tibber, CTEK, Easee, Tesla — and we're constantly adding more. One .lua per device, hot-reloadable, no build step.

Mix any vendors

A Ferroamp meter, a Sungrow battery and an Easee charger — three different makers — run side by side as one coordinated system. Most platforms lock you to a single brand. 42W treats your house as the boundary, not the warranty.

EV & V2X aware

When the car charges, home batteries stop discharging into it — no round-tripping through two battery systems. V2X chargers (Ferroamp DC2, Ambibox) emit bidirectional vehicle power without confusing stationary storage. Easee + OCPP 1.6J central system.

Watchdog

Any driver silent > 60 s flips offline and receives DefaultMode. Each driver implements an autonomous fallback — Sungrow reverts to self-consumption, Ferroamp publishes auto. Safe 02:00 crashes.

Home Assistant

MQTT autodiscovery. Point it at your Mosquitto, sensors and controls appear in HA automatically. Also ships as a Home Assistant OS add-on if you'd rather not run a separate Pi.

Long-term history

SQLite hot store keeps 14 days at 5 s resolution. Daily rolloff to zstd-compressed Parquet, dictionary-encoded per metric. A full year fits in a few GB. Query your house.

Remote access passkeys · p2p

Reach your house from anywhere at home.fortytwowatts.com — secured with passkeys (no passwords) over a peer-to-peer connection, so your data never sits in a vendor cloud. No port forwarding, no VPN. An ftw-pair MCP bridge can even let Claude help debug your install from the other side.

Push notifications ntfy

Subscribe to a topic on your phone, pick events to track (driver offline / recovery), set per-event thresholds and cooldowns. Provider registry; adding Pushover or Slack is one Go file.

Self-tuning batteries

The selftune package injects known step commands, observes the response, and fits ARX(1) parameters. Bootstraps the cascade controller from first principles. No calibration wizard required.

Mobile app soon

The Sourceful Energy mobile app is getting 42W support — pair your Pi over a passkey-secured p2p link, watch live flows, flip planner modes, get push alerts. iOS and Android. Coming soon.

// planner modes

Three modes. One brain.

Same MPC, same self-learning forecasts, same clamps. What changes is the objective. Pick a mode in the UI; the planner re-solves at the next 15-minute boundary. Few open systems offer true passive arbitrage at all — that's where we're unique.

SELFSelf-consumption

Never import to charge. Never export from battery. Just cover your own load. The safe default — what the inverter does on its own, only smoother and meter-aware.

PASSIVEPassive arbitrage

Never imports to charge — works your own PV surplus only. If exporting the surplus right now beats storing it, it exports now; if the evening peak pays more, it waits and discharges then. Your roof pays back faster, the meter never spins backwards on purpose, and few open systems do this at all.

ACTIVEActive arbitrage

Full price optimisation: import when cheap, export when profitable, within fuse and SoC limits. The MPC values terminal SoC at the import–export spread and blends forecasts by confidence so a single bad prediction can't lock in a bad trade.

// design principles

Physics before code.

Every rule exists because ignoring it cost us something. These are the ones we write down.

01Local over cloud

Cloud APIs answer in 2–5 seconds. Grid frequency balances every one. The control path lives on the device; the cloud is opt-in.

02One binary, no CGo

Pure Go, statically linked for linux/arm64. Embeds Lua 5.1 (gopher-lua) so drivers need no toolchain. scp and run.

03The site sign convention is law

Positive W = energy into site. Sign flips happen only at the driver boundary. Any module above drivers violating this is always a bug.

04Robust before feature-rich

Works 100% of the time before it works 110% of the time. Seven clamps, each guarding a named failure mode. Watchdogs. Safe-state on staleness.

05Hardware-stable identity

Devices keyed by make:serial, else mac:<arp>, else endpoint. Renames don't orphan trained model state. Your battery remembers itself.

06Confidence-weighted optimism

The MPC blends forecasts toward horizon mean: eff = conf·real + (1−conf)·mean. Won't lock in expensive trades on a single ML prediction.

07Simple over clever

If you're proud of how clever it is, simplify it. The DP solves in 0.6 ms because it's a DP, not a MILP with a branch-and-cut rodeo.

08Share and enjoy

MIT licensed. Drivers are Lua. Issues are on GitHub. Design is open. Network effects, not lock-in.

// why it's different

It handles real homes — and bends when you want it to.

Plenty of projects can blink an LED when the sun comes out. The hard part is running a whole house — reliably, for years, on whatever mix of hardware you already own. That's the part 42W is actually good at.

01Real systems, for real

Not a lab demo with one battery. 42W runs whole homes — several inverters, batteries, meters and chargers from different brands at once — making a safe, sensible decision every few seconds, right through the depths of winter.

02Most of it just works

Pick your hardware, run the setup wizard, and it starts coordinating your house. The defaults are the ones we'd run in our own homes — safe battery limits, sane fallbacks, a planner that just optimises. You get value on day one without touching a single setting.

03Yours to customise

When you do want to tinker, everything is open and in plain text. Drivers are small Lua files anyone can read or extend, the planner modes are one click in the UI, and every limit is a setting you control — not a locked vendor decision. Easy to bend, hard to break.

// origin

Born from frustration.

We spent too many evenings trying to get a Ferroamp to talk to a Sungrow, a Sungrow to talk to an Easee, and none of them talking to a sane optimiser. Every vendor has a cloud. Every cloud has a latency floor. The grid balances every second — a 2-second round trip to someone's data centre in Frankfurt is not a control loop. It's a rumor.

So we wrote 42W. A single binary you can scp to a Raspberry Pi. Drivers in plain Lua so anyone who can read a register map can add an inverter in an afternoon. Physics at the edge, price-shaving at the planner, and the cloud is a nice-to-have — not a dependency.

It works. Real homes are running it through real winters. Dozens of Lua drivers, three planner modes, EV and V2X awareness, a setup wizard, a Home Assistant add-on, push notifications, and a passkey-secured, peer-to-peer remote-access bridge so we can help debug your install without a VPN.

We know this stuff at Sourceful. Much of the design came from hard-won experience coordinating distributed energy for real customers. 42W is what happens when that experience meets a homeowner who wants it on their terms.

// get started

Four ways in.

Pick the shortest path to your house. All four end up at the same web UI on port 8080 with the same setup wizard.

SD card image easiest

Flash 42w-rpi4-arm64.img.xz to a card with Raspberry Pi Imager or balenaEtcher, boot, open http://42w.local/. A 42w-setup captive portal handles WiFi if you didn't pre-configure it.

One-shot Docker curl | bash

Existing Pi, Debian, or Ubuntu box. The installer pulls the multi-arch image from GHCR and starts the container. Idempotent — re-run to upgrade.

Home Assistant add-on HA OS

Already running Home Assistant OS or Supervised? Install 42W as a supervisor add-on. Maintained by Erik at erikarenhill/ha-addon-forty-two-watts.

From source Go 1.26+

Clone, make dev for sim, make build-arm64 + scp for a Pi. For people who want to add a driver or hack the planner.

# Pi or Linux box, one shot
$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frahlg/forty-two-watts/master/scripts/install.sh | bash
$ open http://<your-pi>:8080/setup

# Source build with simulators
$ git clone https://github.com/frahlg/forty-two-watts && cd forty-two-watts
$ make dev          # sim-ferroamp + sim-sungrow + the app
$ open http://localhost:8080
So long, and thanks for all the kWh.
