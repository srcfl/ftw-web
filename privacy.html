FTW apps, Cloud and the FTW plugin

Privacy

Updated 28 November 2026

Your box keeps the energy record; your phone keeps a recent copy. The app reaches your box through FTW Cloud, which carries the connection encrypted end to end and cannot read it. While FTW Cloud is on, your box also sends it a readable copy of your home, as described below; turning FTW Cloud off stops it. The app does not track you across apps or sites. The project statistics are described below.

This page covers the FTW app for iPhone and Apple Watch, FTW Cloud, and the FTW plugin for AI assistants such as Grok Bot, Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT and Codex, including its MCP connection, and the news and box waitlist sign-ups on this website. Sourceful Labs AB, Sweden, makes the app and runs FTW Cloud. The FTW box is software you run yourself; its documentation describes what it sends.

What stays on your box

Your box reads your meters, solar, battery and charger, keeps the history and makes the plan. That record stays on the box. The app asks the box for what it shows and sends the box what you choose, such as a charging goal.

What the app keeps on your phone

  • The keys that let this phone talk to your box, in the iPhone keychain. They stay on this phone and are not backed up to iCloud.
  • The last readings, prices and car level, so the app can show your home before it reconnects. Each one keeps the time it was read.
  • A copy of the prices and car level that the widgets read. Widgets read nothing else.

Delete the app to remove what it keeps. The box owner can also withdraw a phone's access from the box.

Passkeys

When you pair, the app creates a passkey for app.ftw.energy in your password manager, for example iCloud Keychain. The passkey guards a copy of the key this phone uses with your box. Your password manager keeps the passkey; Sourceful never receives it. The app sends no copy of your keys to Sourceful.

FTW Cloud

The app and the watch reach your box through FTW Cloud at cloud.ftw.energy, which runs in Stockholm, Sweden, on Amazon Web Services. Your box connects to FTW Cloud once you pair a phone. The app and the box encrypt the connection end to end (Noise protocol) before it leaves them. FTW Cloud has no key for it and cannot read or change what it carries.

To carry the connection FTW Cloud sees:

  • a fixed id for your home, made from a key your box holds;
  • the IP addresses of your box and your phone, while they are connected;
  • the list of devices your box allows, which the box signs: each device's id, public key and role;
  • when you connect, for how long, and the size and timing of encrypted messages.

On disk FTW Cloud keeps your home id, your box's public key, the signed device list, the phones you have suspended, the phones registered for notifications, the hashes of access tokens, when to tell you that your box has gone quiet and, under the current terms, the hourly statistics described below; for assistants you approve, it also keeps what the Assistants section lists. It keeps none of the traffic and logs no addresses. We use what it sees to carry your connection and, while the readable copy is on, to serve it as described next.

The readable copy

Pairing a phone, or accepting the FTW Cloud terms on your box's own page, turns FTW Cloud on, and with it a readable copy of your home. The copy has no switch of its own. While FTW Cloud is on, your box sends it:

  • live power readings and how fresh they are;
  • the charger and car state, such as the car's charge level and charging goal;
  • electricity prices;
  • events such as a lost connection;
  • the last 48 hours of power history for grid, solar, battery and home, in five-minute steps.

Hourly project statistics travel with the same grant; see Project statistics below. The grant your box gives FTW Cloud also covers the plan and device health, but the box does not send those today. Your box replaces device, charger and car names with codes; your phone gets the names from your box over the encrypted connection.

FTW Cloud uses the copy to show your home faster, to give your watch and widgets a reading, to show recent history when your box cannot answer, and to send you notifications through Apple's push service. It holds the copy in memory only, never on disk. It drops the readings an hour after your box goes quiet. It keeps each part of the history until 48 hours after that part ends, and drops all of it within a day after your box goes quiet.

To stop the copy, turn FTW Cloud off: on your box under Settings → FTW app → Turn FTW Cloud off, or in the app on the owner's phone, under About FTW → FTW Cloud. Your box then asks FTW Cloud to delete your home, and the copy with it, as soon as it reaches FTW Cloud. Phones and assistants lose remote access, and the box keeps working locally. Pairing a phone again turns FTW Cloud back on.

The copy is not encrypted end to end. Someone in full control of the server could read it while it is held. Sourceful has no tool for staff to read it.

The retired relay

The separate relay at relay.ftw.energy is retired. Current connections use FTW Cloud. Older apps or boxes may need an update.

No ads or tracking across services

The app has no advertising and does not track you across apps or websites. It talks to FTW Cloud and Apple's push service. The project statistics and the Cloud terms are described below.

The camera reads the pairing code from your box and nothing else.

Apple

Apple runs the App Store and TestFlight under its own privacy policy. If you share analytics with app developers in your iPhone settings, Apple sends us crash reports and usage numbers that do not name you. If you test through TestFlight, Apple shares your name, email address, crash reports and any feedback you send with us.

Assistants and the FTW plugin

You can let an assistant, such as Grok Bot, Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT or Codex, read your home through FTW Cloud at cloud.ftw.energy/mcp, and act on it if you allow that too. Nothing reaches an assistant until you approve it, on your phone with Face ID or on your box's own page with the house password. It reads by default; control is a separate choice. Control lasts minutes at a time, within limits your box sets, and your box asks you before any lasting change. Access lasts as long as you choose, from a day up to a year, and you can renew it or end it at any time.

This connection is not end to end between you and your box. The assistant runs outside your home, so FTW Cloud opens the encrypted connection to your box for it. FTW Cloud makes a key for each assistant you approve and keeps it sealed by Amazon's key service (AWS KMS), which only that part of FTW Cloud may use. FTW Cloud reads the assistant's questions and your box's answers while it passes them on, holds them in memory only, and keeps none of them. Sourceful does not use them to train AI models. When a request fails, it logs the tool's name, not what was read.

For each assistant, FTW Cloud keeps its name and web address as the assistant gives them, the public half of its key, which home it may reach, what it may do and until when, and hashes of its access tokens. Your box keeps a record of what each assistant read and did for 90 days.

FTW Cloud sends your phones a short notice through Apple's push service when an assistant gets access, asks for a change or is about to lose access. The notice that an assistant got access names it.

What you let an assistant read goes to the company that runs it, such as xAI, Anthropic, OpenAI or Anysphere (Cursor), under that company's own privacy policy. Sourceful does not control what it does with it.

In whichever assistant you use it, the FTW plugin sends the company that runs the assistant the answers to the tools you use, such as current power, battery and car state, past power readings, plans, device health, and redacted logs and settings. FTW Cloud receives the tool name and arguments needed for the request; the plugin does not ask for your full conversation history. That company handles what it receives under its own privacy policy and your account settings: xAI and Anysphere for Grok Bot and Cursor, Anthropic for Claude, and OpenAI for ChatGPT and Codex. Their retention and deletion controls are separate from FTW's. Ending access in FTW does not delete an earlier conversation in the assistant.

The public plugin uses named tools and does not offer arbitrary API reads or backup exports. Other, separately authorized technical assistant connections can read exact technical identifiers, network addresses and exports; some backups contain saved credentials. Grant and use only the access needed for your task.

Read the FTW plugin terms and the plugin's source. For ChatGPT, read FTW for ChatGPT, its terms and support information.

Access for Sourceful support, which you will be able to turn on for a support case, is not available yet. We will update this page before it is.

Project statistics are part of FTW Cloud

Hourly collection started on 8 November 2026. The current Cloud terms, ftw-cloud-2026-10-v3, include project statistics for everyone who uses FTW Cloud. There is no separate statistics switch. You read and accept the terms once, when you pair a phone or the web app with your box, or on the box itself; your box does not ask again. Earlier Cloud or statistics choices do not accept these terms. Without FTW Cloud your box sends no statistics, hourly or daily. If you do not want to contribute, use FTW locally without Cloud.

Earlier versions of FTW could share statistics from a box without Cloud. Updated Core does not: such a box sends no further report, and a statistics-only connection to FTW Cloud ends, so Cloud deletes its unpublished reports. Local measurement, planning and control keep working.

The statistics policy is ftw-project-stats-2026-10-v3. Your box sends recorded energy for each closed hour: grid import and export, solar generation, battery and vehicle charge and discharge. It also sends configured battery capacity, battery and charger power limits, and solar capacity. Values are rounded to 10 Wh or W. No names, addresses, coordinates, device ids or vehicle ids are included. Configured limits do not prove physical control, and missing readings are not zero. Hours before you accept the terms are not uploaded.

Sourceful uses these values to improve FTW planning and publish group totals, not for advertising or decisions about a household. Reports are not anonymous: FTW Cloud links a random statistics id to your home so it can delete them. The retention limit is 48 hours from the recorded hour.

Someone with full control of the server can read them. Staff cannot browse household reports in a web interface. A named operator can export one home's statistics offline for a verified data rights request; this requires stopping the Cloud service. A verified rights request can also restrict reporting and Cloud access while the request is handled.

The legal basis for people who use FTW Cloud is Sourceful's legitimate interest in improving FTW and publishing group totals (GDPR article 6(1)(f)). You can object at any time by writing to support@sourceful.energy, and we assess each objection individually.

Public energy cells need at least 20 contributing homes, with no home above 30 percent of a sum. The stats page receives only group values. Comparing totals over time can still create a risk of identifying a contributor. To stop contributing, turn FTW Cloud off. Cloud deletes unpublished reports when it receives your box's request; if the box is offline, this happens when it reconnects. Already published group totals remain. Leaving Cloud also ends remote app and assistant access.

The separate daily report describes the FTW version, each driver that delivered readings and its version, battery size to the nearest kWh, price zone and time since installation. It contains no stable box id. No relay hides your address yet: the report goes straight to FTW Cloud, which sees the address it comes from while receiving it, reduces reports to daily totals and stores no individual report or source address. Public daily totals count every report, from one upward, and are published per field, never per box. The daily average estimates reporting active installations, not unique people or all-time installations. Updated Core sends this report only while FTW Cloud is on; older versions use their earlier statistics setting.

FTW news and the FTW box waitlist

On this website you can give us your email address for FTW news, for the FTW box waitlist, or both. We store the address, the list you joined, your country if you chose one, which consent you gave and when. We store nothing else from the form, and no IP address. We use the address only for that list: news about FTW, or an email when FTW boxes go on sale, with the price and shipping. We do not sell it or share it for advertising.

The legal basis is your consent. You can leave a list at any time with the link at the bottom of every email, or by emailing support@sourceful.energy. We then delete your address from that list. So that we do not email you again by mistake, we keep a one-way hash of it: a fingerprint we can match a new sign-up against, but cannot turn back into the address. We delete the waitlist when the box sale ends.

The addresses are kept in a Cloudflare D1 database in the EU that Sourceful controls; Cloudflare processes them on our behalf. We send the emails with Utskick, our own open source newsletter sender, and they are delivered by Amazon Simple Email Service from Amazon Web Services' Frankfurt region in Germany; Amazon Web Services processes the address and the email on our behalf. When you click a link in one of our emails, the click passes through our own server first, which records which link it was and when, and whether the click looks like a mail server's automatic link check rather than a person. To tell, it looks at the network the click comes from and the name your browser gives, and keeps neither, nor your IP address. FTW emails have no tracking image, so we do not record whether you opened them. If an email cannot be delivered or is reported as spam, we stop sending to that address.

Your rights

Sourceful Labs AB (reg. no. 559382-0458), Kalmar, Sweden, is responsible for the data FTW Cloud and the relay handle. Under the GDPR you can ask what we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, and object to how we use it. FTW Cloud holds no name, email address or street address for you, but your home id and IP addresses count as personal data. To ask, email support@sourceful.energy or use Sourceful's contact page. You can also complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY).

The app is not aimed at children.

Changes

When the app changes what it does with data, we change this page first and update the date at the top. The history of this page is public on GitHub.
